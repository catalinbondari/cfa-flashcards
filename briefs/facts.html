Ethics 6

1A member leaves Firm A for Firm B with Firm A's permission to take her old research report, updates it and publishes it. She then rebuilds the supporting records from sources she got at Firm A. Violation?

Yes, but only for rebuilding the records from Firm A's sources (V(C) Record Retention). Updating and publishing the report was fine.

Records must be re-created at the new firm from public sources or directly from the company, not from the old employer's sources or from memory.

Hook: new firm, new records.

2A bank trust officer gets a request from a party headquarters for a client's holdings, saying local law requires it. Her best first step is to:

Check with her compliance department (or legal) on what the law actually requires before disclosing anything (III(E) Confidentiality).

Hook: a claimed legal duty is not a verified one; do not send it and tell someone afterwards.

3Under III(E), client information may be disclosed when:

The client permits it, the law requires it, or the information concerns illegal activity.

Hook: permit, law, crime.

4The investment banking department asks a portfolio manager to recommend the underwritten stock to her clients, but she does not think it is a buy at the current price. She should most likely:

Refuse (ignore the request). I(B) Independence and Objectivity: internal pressure must not shape her opinion.

She recommends it only once fundamentals and price make it attractive.

5An analyst disagrees with her team's buy recommendation on a rights issue. To stay within the Standards, which action is LEAST appropriate?

Issuing her own contradictory report to clients. IV(A) Loyalty: it confuses clients and embarrasses the firm.

Leaving her name on the report, or asking for her name to be removed (dissociating), are both acceptable.

6Two co-authors start a report. One is reassigned, and the other finishes it, changes her conclusion and drops her name. Violation?

No. V(A) Diligence: a member may rely on another's research if that person was diligent and thorough.

Removing the name of the analyst who disagrees is a legitimate way to handle the difference.

Quant 9

7Which return measure is the IRR of the investor's cash flows, and how do you set up the cash flows?

Money-weighted return (MWR). Money added = negative flow, money withdrawn = positive, and the ending value is a final positive flow.

TWR instead chains (1 + r) for each sub-period and takes the root.

8A fund earns a high return on a small base, then receives a large inflow just before a weaker year. Compare MWR with TWR.

MWR < TWR. MWR weights by money invested, so the weak year carries most of the money. TWR ignores the size and timing of flows.

Hook: big money in the weak year drags MWR below TWR.

9An annuity's first payment comes two years from today. The annuity PV formula gives the value at which date?

t = 1, one period before the first payment. Discount that value back one more year to get the PV today.

Hook: annuity PV always lands one period before its first payment.

10A cash flow arrives at the end of Year 10. What N gives its value at the end of Year 5?

N = 5: the year the cash arrives minus the year you are standing in. Not 10, not 6.

Hook: N is the distance between two dates, not the later date.

11For two unequal positive numbers, how do the arithmetic, geometric and harmonic means relate?

GM² = AM × HM, and AM > GM > HM. The geometric mean is the middle one.

Hook: GM² = AM × HM.

12The target downside deviation is calculated as:

The square root of [the sum of (return minus target)² for returns at or below the target, divided by (n − 1)], where n counts ALL observations.

Measure from the target, not from the mean. Returns above the target contribute zero but still count in n.

13A contingency table has r rows and c columns. The chi-square independence test has how many degrees of freedom?

(r − 1)(c − 1).

Hook: drop one row and one column.

14In hypothesis testing, what is a test statistic used for?

To make the statistical decision: compare it with the critical value and reject or fail to reject H0.

It does not estimate a parameter (that is a sample statistic), and it does not set the significance level (chosen in advance).

15The coefficient of variation is calculated as:

σ ÷ mean: risk per unit of return.

Not σ ÷ √n (that is the standard error of the mean).

Economics 8

16Many firms selling differentiated products best describes which market structure, and how does it differ from an oligopoly?

Monopolistic competition: many firms, differentiated products, low barriers to entry.

Oligopoly: few firms, high barriers to entry, close substitutes, firms interdependent.

Hook: many + differentiated = monopolistic competition; few + high barriers = oligopoly.

17In a non-colluding oligopoly with a price leader, the market price is determined by the:

Dominant firm's own demand schedule: it sets output where its MR = MC, and the followers accept that price.

Not the whole market's aggregate demand curve.

18Which fiscal tools are automatic stabilizers?

Taxes (income tax, VAT) and transfer payments (unemployment and other social benefits). They change with output without any new legislation.

Recession: tax revenue falls, benefits rise, deficit widens, downturn is cushioned. Capital spending and spending on goods are discretionary.

19The money supply most likely increases when a central bank:

Buys bonds (open market purchase or repo), cuts the policy rate or lowers the reserve requirement.

Hook: selling bonds, raising the policy rate or raising the reserve requirement all shrink it.

20Under Ricardian equivalence, a deficit-financed tax cut leads households to:

Raise their saving by about the amount of the deficit, expecting higher future taxes. Aggregate demand is unchanged.

Hook: private saving goes UP to offset government dissaving, never down.

21Substantial government borrowing is LESS of a concern when:

Most of the debt is owed internally, to the country's own citizens.

It is more of a concern when the private sector is crowded out to make room for the debt.

22The USD/EUR rate (USD per 1 EUR) rises from 1.10 to 1.20. What happened to the USD?

The USD depreciated by 8.33%. The EUR (the base) appreciated by 1.20 ÷ 1.10 − 1 = 9.09%.

Hook: quote rises = base up. For the other currency flip: old ÷ new − 1 = 1.10 ÷ 1.20 − 1.

23A neutral nominal policy rate equals:

The real trend rate of economic growth plus the target inflation rate.

Do not forget the inflation target.

FSA 13

24What are the six steps of the financial statement analysis framework?

1 Articulate the purpose and context. 2 Collect input data. 3 Process data. 4 Analyze and interpret data. 5 Develop and communicate conclusions and recommendations. 6 Follow up.

25In which FSA framework step are an investment's inherent risks most appropriately discussed?

Step 5: develop and communicate conclusions and recommendations.

26Which items go through OCI rather than the income statement (IFRS)?

Five: FX translation adjustments, FVOCI gains and losses, effective cash flow hedge gains and losses, pension remeasurements, revaluation surplus. Anything else goes through P&L.

Hook: five members, everything else is P&L.

27Under the IFRS revaluation model, where does an upward revaluation go, where does a downward one go, and what happens to leverage?

Upward: OCI (revaluation surplus), unless it reverses an earlier P&L loss. Downward: P&L, unless it reverses an earlier surplus (that part hits OCI first).

Assets and equity rise by the same amount, so financial leverage (assets ÷ equity) falls.

28A company buys a business for less than the fair value of its net identifiable assets. How is the difference reported, versus a normal goodwill case?

Bargain purchase: the excess of fair value over the price is a pretax gain in P&L in the period of the acquisition (not OCI, not goodwill).

Goodwill = purchase price − fair value of net identifiable assets (assets − liabilities), when positive.

29Under US GAAP, how does a lessee report a long-term operating lease?

One straight-line lease expense on the income statement, and the whole payment in CFO. ROU asset and lease liability sit on the balance sheet at the PV (discounted) of payments.

Hook: operating lease = one line, all in CFO. Finance leases (and IFRS) = depreciation + interest, principal in CFF.

30Taxes payable are calculated from which figure, and how does that relate to income tax expense?

Taxable income (the tax return). Tax expense is on accounting profit: tax expense = taxes payable + ΔDTL − ΔDTA.

Payable > expense means the company paid ahead, creating a DTA.

31An increase in the inventory valuation allowance (a write-down) most likely does what to revenue, profit and ratios?

Revenue unchanged, expense up, net income down. Working capital turnover rises (revenue ÷ average working capital, and working capital falls). Days of inventory on hand and interest coverage fall.

Hook: the denominator shrinks, revenue stays.

32Which note disclosure helps an analyst judge the liquidity of inventory?

The split between work in progress and finished goods: finished goods are ready to sell, so they are more liquid than WIP.

33A company buys a patent externally, while a rival builds a similar one in-house. Compare cash flow classification.

The buyer reports a higher investing outflow (CFI). The developer's costs are operating outflows (CFO).

Hook: bought = CFI, built = CFO.

34Revenue is 54.4 and accounts receivable fall from 28.6 to 20.5. How do you find cash collected from customers?

Cash collected = revenue + decrease in accounts receivable (or − the increase). Here 54.4 + 8.1 = 62.5.

Hook: AR down means more cash collected than revenue.

35In the 5-step DuPont, which two terms together make the pretax margin, and what is the trap?

Interest burden (EBT ÷ EBIT) × EBIT margin = pretax margin (EBT ÷ revenue). Use one or the other, never both.

ROE = tax burden × pretax margin × asset turnover × financial leverage.

36In a period of rising prices, FIFO versus LIFO: which gives the higher inventory, net income, taxes, equity and CFO?

FIFO gives higher inventory, net income, taxes and equity. COGS is lower, D/E is lower, CFO is lower (more tax paid).

Hook: FIFO = Fat, Slow Taxpayer.

Corporate issuers 6

37What are the four steps of the capital allocation process?

Generate ideas, analyze individual proposals, create the firm-wide capital budget, then monitor decisions and conduct post-audits.

Hook: idea, analysis, planning, post-audit.

38In the capital allocation process, which of these should a company do: use sunk costs, assume no new competitors, focus on short-term EPS, or include opportunity costs?

Include opportunity costs and expect competitor responses. Ignore sunk costs. Ignore the short-term hit to EPS, ROE or net income: decide on NPV and incremental after-tax cash flows.

Include externalities such as cannibalization. Financing costs belong in the discount rate, not in the cash flows.

39Name the six board committees and what each does.

Audit (financial reporting, controls, auditor), governance (governance code and practices), remuneration or compensation (executive pay), nomination (who sits on the board), risk (risk appetite), investment (major investments and M&A).

Hook: the nominating committee keeps board composition aligned with governance principles.

40Under Modigliani-Miller without taxes, what happens to the cost of equity, the WACC and firm value as D/E rises?

Cost of equity rises (equity holders bear more risk). WACC and firm value stay unchanged.

With taxes: WACC falls and value rises (tax shield), so 100% debt is optimal in theory. The trade-off theory (distress costs) gives an interior optimum.

41Under the pecking order theory, what is the order of financing?

Internally generated funds first, then new debt, then new equity last (information asymmetry).

Hook: own cash, debt, equity.

42Which business growth pattern is described as more users attract more merchants, which attract more users?

A multi-sided network (network effects), which can grow exponentially.

Not crowdsourcing.

Portfolio management 9

43An investor wants to avoid companies that pollute. Which IPS section covers this?

Unique circumstances: beliefs, values and ESG preferences that materially affect portfolio composition.

Constraints are: liquidity, time horizon, tax, legal and regulatory, unique circumstances.

44Which IPS section explains how to keep the IPS current and respond to contingencies, which sets rules on leverage, derivatives and excluded assets, and which assigns roles to client, custodian and managers?

Procedures (keep current, contingencies). Investment guidelines (leverage, derivatives, exclusions). Statement of duties and responsibilities (client, custodian, managers).

45Distinguish liquidity risk, settlement risk and solvency risk.

Liquidity risk: having to accept a significant price concession to sell an asset. Settlement risk: the counterparty fails to deliver or pay when the trade settles. Solvency risk: the entity cannot meet its obligations.

Hook: liquidity = price discount, settlement = delivery failure, solvency = cannot pay.

46Classify: central bank rate changes, a recession, a strike at one firm, a product recall.

Systematic (market-wide, cannot be diversified): central bank rate changes, recession, inflation. Nonsystematic (firm-specific, diversifiable): the strike, the product recall.

Hook: the market feels it = systematic; one company feels it = nonsystematic.

47Which behavioral biases help explain the momentum anomaly?

Availability bias, hindsight bias and loss aversion (faulty learning from recent experience).

Not status quo and not anchoring and adjustment.

48Emotional biases versus cognitive biases: how do they differ and which are harder to correct?

Emotional biases come from feelings and intuition and are harder to correct (loss aversion, overconfidence, self-control, status quo, endowment, regret aversion). Cognitive biases come from faulty reasoning and are easier to correct with information and education.

Cognitive examples: anchoring, confirmation, representativeness, availability, framing, mental accounting, hindsight.

49Define status quo bias, endowment bias and regret aversion bias.

Status quo: prefers to leave things unchanged or take the default. Endowment: values an asset more because he owns it. Regret aversion: avoids acting (or acts only with the crowd) to avoid regret from a bad outcome.

All three are emotional biases.

50An optimal risky portfolio has E(R) 15%, σ 20%, and the risk-free rate is 5%. A risk-seeking investor on the CAL most likely:

Borrows at the risk-free rate and puts more than 100% in the optimal risky portfolio (here, borrow 25% and invest 125%).

Hook: risk-seeking = right of the optimal portfolio; risk-averse = lends part of wealth.

51For an equally weighted portfolio of very many assets, the portfolio variance approaches:

The average covariance (here 0.01 with average variance 0.04, not 0.04 and not 0.05).

Variance = (1 ÷ n) × average σ² + [(n − 1) ÷ n] × average covariance. Own variance diversifies away.

Equity 9

52Which industry classification system covers private companies, non-profits and government entities?

TRBC (Refinitiv Business Classification). GICS and ICB cover public companies only.

53Which pooled product trades furthest from its NAV, and why? Define NAV.

The closed-end fund: fixed share count, so price can sit at a premium or discount to NAV. Open-end funds issue and redeem at NAV. ETFs trade close to NAV because of creation and redemption by authorized participants.

NAV per share = (assets − liabilities) ÷ shares outstanding.

54Compared with market value, the book value of a company's equity is most likely:

More directly influenced by management decisions, because management's choices flow through net income into book equity.

Market value reflects investors' expectations of future cash flows. Book equity = net income ÷ ROE, not multiplied.

55Which valuation approach does not need an estimate of the required return on the share?

A price multiple model.

The Gordon growth model and FCFE models both discount at RR.

56The value of a non-callable, non-convertible perpetual preferred share is:

Dividend ÷ RR, where RR is the required return (not the dividend rate, not g).

57Which market-efficiency form does each test address: serial correlation of returns, event studies of public announcements, and performance of insiders?

Serial correlation and technical trading rules: weak form. Public announcements and fundamental analysis: semi-strong. Insider or private information: strong form.

Hook: past prices, public info, private info.

58In an efficient market, asset prices react to the release of:

Unexpected information only (the surprise element). Expected information is already in the price.

59Compared with public equity, what is a feature of operating in the private equity market?

Management can better adopt a long-term focus, without quarterly earnings pressure.

Public markets offer easier access to capital raising.

60Distinguish execution, validity and clearing instructions.

Execution: how to trade (market or limit). Validity: how long the order stays active (day, good-till-cancelled, immediate-or-cancel). Clearing: how the trade settles.

Hook: how, when, settle.

Fixed income 12

61Which duration measure is most appropriate for a callable bond?

Effective duration. Cash flows depend on the call decision, so there is no well-defined YTM, and yield-based Macaulay and modified duration do not apply.

62Which measure captures the price sensitivity of a bond to a non-parallel shift (a change in shape) of the benchmark curve?

Key rate duration (partial duration): sensitivity to a change at one maturity point, which shows shaping risk.

Effective duration assumes a parallel shift.

63For an option-free fixed-rate bond, what changes the spread and what changes the benchmark yield?

Spread: the issuer's credit risk and the bond's liquidity. Benchmark yield: expected inflation and the expected real rate.

Hook: spread = credit + liquidity; benchmark = inflation + real rate.

64A bond's price rises while the government benchmark yield rises. What happened to the spread?

It fell by more than the fall in YTM. Change in spread = change in YTM − change in benchmark yield.

Example: YTM −50 bp with the benchmark +10 bp means the spread fell 60 bp.

65A bond's yield falls. Which way does the price move, and how do duration and convexity each contribute?

Price rises, and both terms add. %ΔP ≈ −ModDur × Δy + ½ × Convexity × (Δy)², and the convexity term is always positive.

Hook: yield down, price up; convexity helps both ways.

66In bankruptcy with losses assessed by priority of claims, which of second-lien debt, subordinated debt and senior unsecured debt has the lowest loss severity?

Second-lien debt: it ranks above both senior unsecured and subordinated debt.

Order: first lien, second lien, senior unsecured, subordinated (paid first = lowest loss).

67In a securitization, who is the depositor, the SPE, the servicer and the trustee?

Depositor (originator): sells the pool of assets. SPE: buys the pool and issues the securities, bankruptcy-remote. Servicer: collects payments from borrowers. Trustee: protects investors' interests and holds the assets for them.

68Which mortgage loan is most likely prime: low LTV first lien, low LTV second lien, or high LTV first lien?

Low LTV first lien. Prime means high-credit-quality borrower, substantial equity and a first lien.

Subprime: lower borrower quality and/or no first lien.

69Which ABS structure typically uses credit tranching as credit enhancement: credit card ABS, a covered bond or an agency mortgage pass-through?

Credit card ABS: subordination, so losses hit the junior tranches first.

Agency pass-through relies on an agency guarantee. Covered bond relies on a cover pool plus recourse to the issuer.

70An investment-grade bond index is usually subdivided by:

Both maturity (short, intermediate, long) and credit rating (AAA, AA, A, BBB).

71What are the three meanings of 'discount' that trap candidates: bank discount rate, FRN discount margin, and trading at a discount?

Bank discount rate: the discount is measured against FACE value (DR = Year/Days × (FV − PV) ÷ FV). FRN discount margin: the spread over the reference rate that prices the FRN at its current price (DM > QM if price < par, DM < QM if price > par). Trading at a discount: price below par, so YTM > coupon rate.

Hook: face, spread, below par.

72Current yield is calculated as:

Annual coupon ÷ flat price. Use the stated annual coupon even if paid semiannually. It is not the YTM.

Derivatives 10

73What replicates a short forward (put-call-forward parity)?

A short call plus a long put, same strike (= the forward price) and same expiry.

Long forward = long call + short put.

Hook: flip both legs to go from long forward to short forward.

74What is a short call replicated by in put-call parity (c = p + S − PV(X))?

Short put + short stock + long risk-free bond (lend PV(X)).

Check: long call = long put + long stock + borrow PV(X). Flip all three legs, including the put.

75The call is overpriced (or the put is underpriced) against put-call parity. What is the arbitrage?

Sell the call, buy the put, buy the stock, and borrow PV(X). The inflows exceed the outflows today, with zero payoff at expiry.

76What is the value of a long forward position at expiration, and of a short?

Long = spot price at expiry − forward price. Short = forward price − spot price at expiry.

Hook: long wants spot above the agreed price.

77Which positions gain if the underlying price falls, and which gain if it rises?

Falls: short forward, short call, long put (bearish trio). Rises: long forward, long call, short put (bullish trio).

78If futures prices are positively correlated with interest rates, futures prices are likely to be:

Higher than forward prices. When prices are high, rates tend to be high too, so long holders reinvest their margin gains at higher rates.

Negative correlation: futures lower than forwards.

79How does a European call's value change with S, X, T, σ and r?

S up: up. X up: down. T up: up. σ up: up. r up: up.

Hook: only the exercise price hurts the call.

80How does a European put's value change with S, X, T, σ and r?

S up: down. X up: up. T up: up (Level I rule). σ up: up. r up: down.

Hook: S and r hurt the put; volatility and time help both.

81What is novation in derivatives clearing?

Replacing the original contract with two contracts in which the clearing house (CCP) becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer.

Counterparty credit risk is then faced with the CCP.

82How is a forward price calculated, and how does it differ from forward value?

Forward price = (spot price − PV of benefits + PV of costs) × (1 + r)^T, where spot is today's price. With no benefits or costs: S0 × (1 + r)^T.

The price is fixed at initiation. The value starts at 0 and moves with the market.

Alternatives 5

83Distinguish a clawback, a high-water mark and a hurdle rate.

Clawback: LPs can reclaim part of the GP's performance fee already paid if later losses occur. High-water mark: no new performance fee until the fund recovers above its prior peak, so clients do not pay twice for the same gains. Hurdle: the minimum return that must be exceeded before a performance fee is earned.

Hard hurdle: fee only on the excess. Soft hurdle: fee on all the return once the hurdle is cleared.

84Debtor-in-possession (DIP) financing is provided to firms that are:

Already in bankruptcy, to fund operations while they reorganize.

Not firms at risk of bankruptcy, and not healthy growth firms.

85Which commodities are hard and which are soft?

Hard: mined or extracted (copper, crude oil). Soft: grown over time (grains, coffee, livestock).

Hook: crude oil = hard, cattle = soft.

86Distinguish economic infrastructure from social infrastructure.

Economic: supports commerce (roads, airports, ports, pipelines, power, communications). Social: supports society (hospitals, schools, prisons).

87Describe Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3 asset valuation under US GAAP.

Level 1: quoted price in an active market for the identical asset. Level 2: observable inputs other than Level 1, such as reliable outside broker quotes. Level 3: unobservable inputs, model-based values.

Hook: market price, broker quote, own model.
